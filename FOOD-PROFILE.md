## 3. Hard rules and preferences

- **No tomatoes.** Never suggest tomatoes, tomato sauce, salsa, pico de gallo, ketchup or tomato-based
  dishes. Offer non-tomato alternatives.
- **Žemaitijos varškė** without a variant = **liesa 0,5 %** (96 kcal, 20 g protein /100 g).
- "Amfora greek yoghurt" = Graikiška Amfora natūralus (assume 3,9 %).
- He **weighs food** and lists every sauce he adds when cooking at home — if a sauce isn't in his
  note, it isn't in the dish.
- Prefers simple, high-protein, low-processing food; likes seeing grams and per-portion nutrition.
- Avoid: processed meat (sausages, ham, "no added E" sausages are still NOVA 4C), mayonnaise-based
  dressings, battered/fried dishes, sweet glazes, tortillas, store pastries.
- Planned small treats are fine (e.g. 1/5 of a cake slice); judge entries relative to targets — don't
  call a good dish "the weakest" just because it scored lowest on a clean day.
- Mild hunger is acceptable; push extra food only when the deficit heads past ~500 kcal.
- **Ultra-processed food (stated 2026-10-23):** avoids UPF where he reasonably can, but it is not a
  hard rule. Prefer NOVA 1–3 ingredients; name any NOVA 4 ingredient in a recipe and offer a
  less-processed swap (e.g. traditionally brewed soy sauce, plain spices instead of stock cubes).

### Cooking for the family (stated 2026-10-23)

- He usually cooks **one dish for the whole family** (him, Ausra, the kids) → default to
  **5–6 portions**, mild and kid-friendly; adults add chili, extra ginger, lemon/lime or soy at the
  table, so put sharp/spicy extras **on the side**.
- Main pot: **Uno Casa enamelled cast-iron Dutch oven**, red, round, stainless-steel lid knob — use
  it for browning in batches, stir-fries, stews, braises and oven bakes. He also has ordinary pots,
  pans and an oven. Size confirmed by Domas from the Amazon listing: **5.7 L (6 qt), sold as
  "30 cm"**. Working rules:
  - preheat on **medium for 3–5 min**; MEDIUM-HIGH is the hottest hob setting to write (enamel
    doesn't like empty high heat, and cast iron holds heat anyway);
  - brown meat in batches of **~400–500 g** so it browns instead of steaming;
  - keep stews/braises to **~4 L** total so they don't boil over;
  - oven: take the silicone handle covers off; the oven-temperature limit isn't verified, so keep
    recipes at **≤ 230 °C**;
  - no cold water into a hot empty pot; wooden/silicone utensils.

### Recipe format (stated 2026-10-23, example: his "Ginger-Garlic Chicken & Rice")

He cooks straight from the recipe and doesn't want to scroll between the ingredient list and the
steps:
1. Title, then one line with portions and character, e.g. "5–6 portions · mild/kid-friendly".
2. **Ingredients** list in g/ml (tsp/tbsp fine for salt and spices); mark optional items "optional".
3. **Instructions** as numbered steps that **repeat every quantity in bold where it is used**
   ("Add **200 g onion + 300 g carrots** and cook for **4 min**") — never just "add the onion".
4. Each cooking step starts with the heat level in caps: **HIGH heat.**, **MEDIUM-HIGH heat.**,
   **MEDIUM heat.**, **LOW heat.**, **OVEN 180 °C.**, **HEAT OFF.**; optional steps start with
   **OPTIONAL —**.
5. Times in every step, plus practical cues and bold warnings (brown meat in 2–3 batches without
   constant stirring; keep rice slightly firm; add 30–50 ml water if it sticks; taste before salting).
6. Nutrition **after** the recipe: whole-pot totals, per 100 g cooked and per portion. Say which
   ingredient versions it was calculated with and how other choices shift it per portion.
7. **No prescribed fat levels or grades** (stated 2026-10-23) — list "beef mince", "milk", "Greek
   yoghurt", "quark/varškė" plainly, not "15 % mince" or "liesa varškė". Where the choice matters, the
   step says what to do for each (e.g. "lean mince, 10 % or less: add 5 g olive oil; fattier: no oil,
   spoon off excess fat"), followed by a short note on what the fattier vs leaner version brings
   (texture, taste, calories) and whether anything else has to be added or changed. Applies to every
   ingredient with grades and to all future recipes.
8. **Shareable by default** (stated 2026-10-23) — recipes must work for anyone. Everything specific to
   Domas (weighing his pot for suvalgiau, the silicone handle covers, his portion, what he usually
   buys) goes into a **step 0 "For Domas only"** so it can be deleted before sharing. For a PDF, make
   two copies: personal (with step 0) and shareable (without).

## 8. How to update this file

1. Work on branch **`claude/project-explanation-ms0hvn`**: `git fetch origin claude/project-explanation-ms0hvn && git checkout claude/project-explanation-ms0hvn && git pull`.
2. **Before** relying on the file, pull so you have the other session's latest edits.
3. Edit the relevant section (preference → §3, new staple → §4, recipe → §5, context → §6, new
   targets after a calibration → §2). Keep it factual and dated.
4. Commit and push right away:
   `git add FOOD-PROFILE.md && git commit -m "Food profile: <what changed>" && git push -u origin claude/project-explanation-ms0hvn`
   (retry on network errors with 2/4/8/16 s backoff). If the push is rejected, `git pull --rebase`
   and push again.
5. Don't put model names, keys or other users' food details into the file.
