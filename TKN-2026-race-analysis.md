## 3. Heart rate

- **The first ~25 min of HR is unreliable.** At 6:50 /km your HR read 126–143, then jumped to 162 within one minute at km 3.7 without any change in pace. A wrist sensor typically locks onto the wrong signal or lags in cool, humid starts. Treat km 1–4 HR as wrong; this also makes the whole-run HR averages slightly low.
- **For km 4–32, HR averaged ~170 for 3.5 hours.** That's very high for a 4-hour effort. Max 185 came on the km 21 climb.
- **Your Garmin HR zones are set too low.** It counted 213 min in "Zone 5" (>156 bpm) and logged "overreaching". A true Zone 5 can't be held for 3.5 h, so your real lactate threshold is probably around 168–172. Run Garmin's lactate-threshold test, or set your zones from a recent hard 30-min effort, so zone data and training-effect labels make sense.
- **Readiness going in was low:** HRV status "Unbalanced" (53 against a baseline of 54–70), resting HR 51 (up from 46), and body battery 35 at the start. You raced on a tired system, and that's part of why HR ran high.

1. Fuelling by km marks, gels on the move: ~80 g/h, no bonk.
2. Caffeine gels in the second half (km 20 and 27).
3. Short stops only at the aid points.
4. Carb loading the day before (~435 g).

**Change**
1. **Start at plan pace.** Run the first 10 km at 7:45–8:00 /km even if it feels too easy. On today's data that would likely have kept km 27–31 near 7:30 instead of 8:00+, for the same or a faster finish.
2. **Fix HR zones**, and wear a chest strap for races if you have one (the first 25 min of wrist HR were wrong).
3. **Train with gels** on every 90+ min run to build gut tolerance and cut the gas.
4. **On carb-load day,** keep it low in fibre and fat: white bread, rice, no chia or pear, and the last big meal by 19:00.
5. **Cadence work:** about 165 spm on easy runs.
6. **Strength training 2× a week**, to protect stride length late in races and to protect lean mass during the weight loss.
7. **Taper sleep:** arrive with body battery above 60. Both nights before were 6.5–7 h; aim for 8.
